Intelligence Report 01 · MLB Draft

Where draft intelligence actually creates value.

A 20-year benchmark across every MLB organization and every draft round asks a deliberately hard question: when does a smaller, model-identified player pool outperform the choices clubs actually made?

22,893 pick episodes 30 organizations 2005–2024 observed
The answer Do not replace the early board.

Preserve premium evaluation in Rounds 1–5. The strongest evidence for this model begins after Round 10, where it improves established-player outcomes for most organizations.

The top-five benchmark

A smaller pool raises the base rate—but only modestly.

The important distinction:

The cohort itself is not the advantage. The identification rules create the separation. That signal is useful, but it is not strong enough to justify replacing obvious premium talent.

Operating implication

One draft needs two different decision systems.

01 · Protect

Rounds 1–5

Keep premium scouting, medical review, role evaluation, signability, and consensus talent at the center. The current model is destructive when used as a wholesale replacement here.

02 · Calibrate

Rounds 6–10

Use the model as a challenge layer, not the final board. Only eight organizations improve in the historical counterfactual.

03 · Deploy

Rounds 11+

Use the model aggressively to surface overlooked players. It improves 25 clubs in Rounds 11–20 and 19 clubs in Rounds 21+.

Method boundary

What this report does—and does not—claim.

  • Established career: 162 MLB games for position players; 20 starts or 80 total innings for pitchers.
  • Strict gate: 324 games for position players; 40 starts or 160 total innings for pitchers.
  • Maturity: team rankings use 2005–2019. The 2020–2024 classes are observed but right-censored.
  • Availability: a modeled candidate must still be unselected at the team's pick and can be used once per team and draft.
  • Not organizational ROI: the benchmark assumes the selected player signs and does not model bonus pools, medicals, roster need, or development quality.
  • Research status: this is retrospective exploratory analysis, not a production draft board or a claim of licensed historical evidence.

The next question
